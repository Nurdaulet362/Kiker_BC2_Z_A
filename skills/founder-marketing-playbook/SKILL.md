# Founder marketing playbook

Turn an existing product into an understandable offer and a small, testable path to early users. Read [source notes](references/source-notes.md) for provenance, evidence limits, and the relevant source sections. This skill synthesizes a resource collection; it does not claim that every linked tactic is proven or appropriate for this product.

## Ground the offer

Read the product brief and inspect the working experience before writing claims. Establish the user, the situation that brings them here, the task they want to complete, the available product behavior, and the next useful action. Distinguish those facts from audience, pricing, and channel hypotheses.

Express the offer in reusable building blocks: audience and trigger; problem; concrete product action; immediate benefit; observable evidence. Keep a lightweight messaging note when several surfaces need consistent copy. A feature, genuine product view, or usable sample can support a benefit before customer testimonials exist.

## Improve the landing experience

- Make the first screen explain who should use this, what they will do, and what they receive. Preserve a distinctive voice when it stays clear to a first-time visitor.
- Match the primary action to the available product. Name the action and state relevant effort, cost, account requirements, or setup near it when known. Preserve a returning user's continue path.
- Tie benefit copy to working evidence: preview the artifact, demonstrate a meaningful result, or explain how feedback is produced. An illustrative scenario must remain visibly illustrative.
- Resolve the few uncertainties that prevent starting. A short explanation, process, or FAQ should answer a real product question; do not add a long standard marketing template by default.
- Make the same primary action reachable after the explanation. Secondary paths should serve users with a different immediate need, such as a shorter guided introduction.

Do not turn missing evidence into fabricated testimonials, adoption counts, conversion improvements, superiority claims, or promised user outcomes. Distinguish an exercise score from validated real-world performance. Describe pricing experiments as experiments rather than available purchases.

## Select an early growth experiment

Use the repository as a menu, not a checklist. Choose the relevant bottleneck: unclear offer, first useful session, audience access, repeated use, or willingness to pay.

For audience access, prefer a small number of plausible channels where the intended users already discuss the problem. Consider useful product demonstrations, founder explanations, listening to existing conversations, or a narrow free tool that leads naturally into the product. A list of launch directories alone is not a distribution strategy. Search-driven acquisition may take longer than direct learning from early users; choose based on the product's time horizon.

For each proposed experiment, record the audience, trigger, hypothesis, artifact, meaningful action to observe, time or effort bound, and decision the result will inform. Use existing measurement where available. For a small audience, observed task completion and specific interviews may be more informative than an underpowered A/B test. Do not claim a conversion lift from a local implementation review.

## Learn from actual use

Separate problem exploration from reactions to a prototype. Ask about concrete past preparation, workarounds, or workflows; then observe someone attempt the task. Use a short, neutral question at the relevant moment to understand a difficulty that behavior alone cannot explain. Record observations separately from interpretations and proposed changes.

Do not label browser-local notes as collected customer research. Drafting outreach or a launch plan does not authorize sending messages, publishing, paid promotion, account creation, or payment collection. Complete authorized local edits and reviewable drafts within the existing scope.

## Deliver and verify

For site changes, check that every new claim matches current behavior and that the main action, continuation, and secondary path still work. For an experiment plan, explain the decision it will inform and what remains unvalidated. Report the actual changes, the relevant source rationale, and the verification performed.
