## What the repository supports

The README is a curated collection for early startup acquisition, not empirical validation of a particular business. Its relevant sections provide these directions:

| README section | Supported direction | Boundary for this skill |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Landing Pages, Messaging and Positioning | Clarify the audience, product, and reasons to choose it; connect to homepage and proof resources. | Use demonstrated benefits; a linked social-proof article does not justify inventing proof. |
| Conversion Rate Optimization | Treat changes as candidate experiments. | Examples of other companies' results are not forecasts for this product. |
| Idea Validation; User Research | Investigate demand and collect feedback from intended users. | A functional demo does not validate demand or willingness to pay. |
| Pricing | Explore value, price testing, and willingness to pay. | A candidate price is not an active offer or validated business model. |
| Social Media Marketing; Places To Launch Your Startup | Consider founder content, listening, and relevant communities. | Verify audience fit and current platform rules before proposing execution. |
| SEO; Free-Tool Marketing | Consider a longer-term discovery channel and useful small tools as acquisition assets. | Do not repeat broad viral-growth claims as expected outcomes. |

The contribution guidelines favor useful, current, personally reviewed resources and discourage indiscriminate promotion. They govern contributions to that repository; they do not authorize external actions here.

## Linked primary articles actually inspected

These pages were read on 2026-11-14. Their content can change independently of the pinned repository.

- [Emily Kramer, MKT1: How to create a more effective homepage](https://newsletter.mkt1.co/p/homepage-copy), published 2022-11-28. Recommends a short messaging guide, a clear audience and problem, benefits paired with product evidence, and an obvious action after the page narrative. Its B2B page structure should be adapted to the user's product rather than applied mechanically.
- [Ian Vanagas, PostHog: How to write great product survey questions](https://posthog.com/product-engineers/product-survey-questions), published 2023-08-23. Connect questions to a research goal, ask near the relevant experience, avoid leading wording, and use a short response format suited to the question.
- [Ian Vanagas, PostHog: An engineer's guide to talking to users](https://posthog.com/newsletter/talk-to-users), published 2024-04-25. Investigate concrete workflows and difficulties, distinguish exploration from solution feedback, and preserve what was learned before choosing changes.

## Example application: Fix the Report

The product brief, demo notes, and `src/simulation/Simulation.tsx` describe a guest sales-operations exercise with two formulas, decision questions, 30 checks, feedback, a downloadable review, and varied retry data. These are usable product facts. Audience demand, hiring outcomes, willingness to pay, and acquisition performance remain unvalidated.

Small applications of the guidance: make assessment preparation explicit in the hero; expose the concrete review and retry outcomes before starting; answer account, prerequisite, and score-meaning questions close to the final action. These are local product judgments informed by the sources, not changes prescribed verbatim by the repository. Preserve the fictional-data label, learner progress, and existing workbook design.
